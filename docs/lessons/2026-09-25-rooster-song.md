# Lesson, 25 Sep 2026: a song about animals

Cleaned from the video-call captions, which caught very little of the Greek, so much of the lesson survives only as Mike's English asides and the teacher's corrections. **[?]** marks Greek reconstructed from context, and **[…]** marks a stretch that could not be recovered. Filler, the singing itself and the chat before and after the lesson are cut.

---

**2:40 · The song**

**Teacher:** You have the words, but you're going to listen, so it can be easier. I'm not giving you the easy version, because you're advanced: you get the real song. Whenever you're ready, fill in the missing words.

*The song is the children's song **Όταν θα πάω, κυρά μου, στο παζάρι** [?]. Each verse adds one animal and its sound.*

**7:36 · Animal words**

**Mike:** A couple got lost there, but I could be sneaky and use answers from the previous section. How is **σκυλάκι** spelled?

**Teacher:** And **γουρουνάκι**, the pig. It's not the animal you like most: it's the one you have to eat, because the one you like is expensive.

**Mike:** What was "cats" again? And what's milk?

**Teacher:** **Γάλα.** In the song all the animals are the small forms. Normally it's **κότα**, but here we have **κοτούλα** [?], and **γάτα** becomes **γατούλα** [?].

**Mike:** The ones I'm not sure about are κόκορας, κότα and πουλί. Parrot, or bird, or something?

**Teacher:** **Η κότα** is the hen. **Ο κόκορας** is… remind me of the name in English?

**Mike:** Rooster. I thought κοτόπουλο was chicken meat.

**Teacher:** It is. **Κοτόπουλο** comes from **κότα**, the hen, and **πουλί**, bird. Πουλιά are all the birds.

**12:32 · What does it say?**

**Teacher:** So what's the story? How would you translate it? It repeats the same line again and again.

**Mike:** Does the θα mean "we"?

**Teacher:** No, no. **Όταν**, when: not the question "when?", but "when" inside a sentence. Who is going?

**Mike:** I am: **όταν θα πάω**, when I go. Then **κυρά μου**, my lady.

**Teacher:** That's the old way to say it. And **στο παζάρι**: to the market. It's the open market.

**Mike:** I will buy… **θα σου αγοράσω** [?], I'll buy you a rooster.

**Teacher:** And the rooster goes **κικιρίκου**. That's all: there's no verb.

**Mike:** In English we'd say "the rooster goes cock-a-doodle-doo", but I'm not seeing a verb here. Is that just because it's a song?

**Teacher:** You're right: there's no verb. And **κάθε πρωί σε ξυπνάει** [?]: it wakes you up every morning. The rest of the song is the same, adding one animal at a time.

**19:20 · Homework: sing it**

**Teacher:** Your homework is to sing the song. I'll send you the version we listened to, and one I really love: some singers from England, I think, performing it like an opera. They're not Greek, but they sing it, with the face of each animal and its proper sound.

**Teacher:** What's the first word you wrote? For taking the dog out, we use **βγάζω** [?].

**Teacher:** That was really good. Well done, Mike. Your homework is to sing the song, and to redo the first page in the past tense.

**What the lesson produced, in correct Greek:**

- Όταν θα πάω, κυρά μου, στο παζάρι… — When I go, my lady, to the market…
- θα σου αγοράσω έναν κόκορα — I'll buy you a rooster
- Κάθε πρωί σε ξυπνάει. — It wakes you up every morning.
- ο κόκορας · η κότα · το πουλί — rooster · hen · bird
- το κοτόπουλο — chicken (to eat): κότα + πουλί
- κότα → κοτούλα · γάτα → γατούλα — small forms, as in the song
- το σκυλάκι · το γουρουνάκι — little dog · little pig
- το γάλα — milk
- βγάζω τον σκύλο βόλτα — I take the dog for a walk
