# Lesson, 27 Oct 2026: how often, continued

Cleaned from the video-call captions. They caught almost none of the Greek, so most of the lesson survives only as Mike's English asides and the teacher's corrections. **[?]** marks Greek reconstructed from context, and **[…]** marks a stretch that could not be recovered. Filler and the chat before and after the lesson are cut. The lesson ended about five minutes early, when the teacher's power went out.

The lesson picked up the picture exercise from 20 Oct: people doing activities, each with a name and how often they do it.

**Mike:** We started the third one, the brushing one. It's in the notes.

**Teacher:** Ο Ντέιβιντ… to brush, or to wash?

**Mike:** Ο Ντέιβιντ… […]

**Teacher:** Perfect. Perfect.

**4:20 · Twice on Monday**

**Mike:** Η Μαρκέτα… Is that supposed to mean she goes twice on Monday, and the rest of the week just in the evening?

**Teacher:** Mhm. Or you can say she goes two times on Monday, and on the other days only in the evening. Whatever you want.

**Mike:** What's the third person of πάω? **Πάει.** And I can't remember the word for gym. I see it so often, because there are tons of gyms around here.

*The gym is **το γυμναστήριο**: **πάει στο γυμναστήριο**.*

**Mike:** Would I just say **δύο** for twice?

**Teacher:** Two times. We don't have one word for it; we have two words: **δύο φορές**.

**Mike:** Did we cover that last time? I usually remember it, but not the specifics.

**Teacher:** Days and months are always in the Target form: **τη Δευτέρα** [?].

**7:40 · From Tuesday to Friday**

**Mike:** Would "from" be **από**? From Tuesday to Friday… And what would "to" be: "from… until"?

**Teacher:** **Μέχρι.** You have it in the notes.

*In full: **από την Τρίτη μέχρι την Παρασκευή** [?].*

**Teacher:** And it's **μία**, only because it's φορά: it's feminine. That's all.

**Mike:** I overthink that so much now that even when I think it should be μία, I second-guess it.

**9:24 · She never drinks coffee**

**Mike:** Πίνω… πίνεις…

**Teacher:** **Πίνει** is the third person. Πίνεις is the second.

**Mike:** Then πίνει is the right one. **Πίνει καφέ**, without the article, because we're talking about coffee in general.

**Teacher:** How would you say "never"? She never drinks coffee. What's the order: "she doesn't drink never coffee", or "she never drinks coffee"?

*Either works: **ποτέ δεν πίνει καφέ**, or **δεν πίνει ποτέ καφέ**. ποτέ still needs δεν with the verb.*

**10:40 · Skiing once a year**

**Mike:** Skiing is such an arbitrary one. I'm trying to find a verb I know that I can use.

**Teacher:** One time, here: μία φορά. And for skiing, she goes for it.

**Mike:** Oh, is it just **σκι**? So it's πάει: **πάει για σκι, μία φορά** […].

**Teacher:** That's it. Perfect. Well done.

*In full: **πάει για σκι μία φορά τον χρόνο** [?].*

**12:20 · Your turn: Mike's week**

*The second half of the exercise: expressions of frequency on the left, to build sentences about Mike's own week.*

**Mike:** The word my brain reached for is **συνήθως**.

**Mike:** **Πάω** […] **Λέο**. No. Oh, the article again.

*With με, a name takes the article in the Target form: **με τον Λέο**.*

**What the lesson produced, in correct Greek:**

- πάει στο γυμναστήριο — she goes to the gym
- δύο φορές τη Δευτέρα — twice on Monday (days take the Target form)
- από την Τρίτη μέχρι την Παρασκευή — from Tuesday to Friday
- μία φορά — once (μία, because η φορά is feminine)
- πίνω · πίνεις · πίνει — I drink · you drink · she drinks
- Πίνει καφέ. — She drinks coffee. (no article: coffee in general)
- Ποτέ δεν πίνει καφέ. — She never drinks coffee. (ποτέ still needs δεν)
- πάει για σκι μία φορά τον χρόνο [?] — she goes skiing once a year
- συνήθως — usually
- με τον Λέο — with Leo (a name takes the article)
