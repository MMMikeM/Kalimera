# Lesson, 20 Oct 2026: the role cards, and how often

Cleaned from the video-call captions, which had mangled most of the Greek, so the Greek is rewritten in Greek script. **[?]** marks Greek reconstructed from context, and **[…]** marks a stretch that could not be recovered. Filler and the chat before and after the lesson are cut.

---

**3:40 · The homework**

**Mike:** The hardest part was reading the picture: the text came out looking like it was from 2005. The homework itself was easy. I looked the words up more than I read them.

**Teacher:** You had to read "the boy"… **ο ανιψιός**, nephew. Μπράβο, good job.

**Mike:** This was easily achievable. There's a sweet spot where homework takes more than ten minutes but less than two hours.

**5:44 · Role cards for Friday**

**Teacher:** Normally we'd do a speaking part, but I'll put it in the chat so you can prepare, and we'll do it on Friday. There's a part A and a part B, and I want you prepared for both roles. What does it say?

**Mike:** Συναντάω μια φίλη… μετά από χρόνια… I understand about a quarter of that. So, a friend. **Μετά** is "after"… Oh: a friend after years.

**Teacher:** You meet them after years.

**Mike:** **Λέμε**… is "νέα" new?

**Teacher:** **Τα νέα**, in the plural: the news.

**Mike:** Oh, we talk about the news. **Της οικογένειάς μας**: of our families.

**Teacher:** Μπράβο, "of our family" is perfect. It's possession.

**Mike:** Now my children are **παντρεμένα**, married. I don't know the last word… Cousin? No. Oh, **εγγόνια**, grandchildren. It kind of makes sense.

**Mike:** Card B has **μας** too. My son is now married and he's expecting a child. **Η κόρη μου** has a son…

**Teacher:** **Εκτός γάμου**: out of marriage. Scandalous!

**Mike:** That's a weird thing to announce: "Hey, news about my family: my daughter has sinned."

**Teacher:** They meet and they gossip. We'll play it on Friday. Be prepared for both roles.

**12:04 · What are they doing?**

*A new exercise: pictures of people doing activities, each with a name and how often they do it.*

**Mike:** When you say the names, is that their names, or the people? I have no idea what yoga is in Greek.

**Teacher:** **Γιόγκα.** It's just γιόγκα. Whole sentence.

**Mike:** Η Γιάννα… κάνει γιόγκα. Is it a tense? I thought it had to be continuous…

**Teacher:** I wish I could see inside your mind! It's easy here: just present. No past. And how would you say "every"?

**Mike:** I have "how often" in my notes…

**Teacher:** **Κάθε**: κάθε φορά, every time. Just κάθε.

**Teacher:** I have a question. Did you say "Γιάννα" or "η Γιάννα"? The pronunciation was very close. With or without the article? We have to use it.

**Mike:** I didn't put an article in.

*With a name, Greek uses the article: **η Γιάννα κάνει γιόγκα**.*

**18:04 · Twice a month**

**Mike:** **Μήνας** is month. So are we saying they play games in January and February?

**Teacher:** No: two times per month, twice a month. For "times" we use **φορά**, and we add the number in front.

**Mike:** That jogs my memory. So **δύο φορές τον μήνα** [?].

**Teacher:** Yes.

**Mike:** I struggle with capital letters so badly.

**Teacher:** Where are the capital letters? They're all names.

**20:08 · με τον παππού του**

**Teacher:** Ο Νάθαν [?] plays… with his grandpa.

**Mike:** I'm trying to remember "play". You mentioned it earlier and I've already forgotten it.

**Teacher:** **Παίζει.** Παίζει PlayStation, or **βιντεοπαιχνίδια**, video games. With his grandpa: you said **με**. What case do we use after με? It's a preposition.

**Mike:** The Target form. **Με τον παππού.**

**Teacher:** Μπράβο.

**Mike:** I need to note that, because it's a mistake I keep making: the Target form after a preposition. Isn't it the same as **από**?

**Teacher:** Mhm. Από and σε, too.

*In full: **ο Νάθαν παίζει βιντεοπαιχνίδια με τον παππού του** [?].*

**24:04 · Brushing teeth**

**Mike:** Using the same pattern, what would "brush" be? I read it in a children's book, and there was both a verb and a noun.

**Teacher:** Not "clean": we say "wash". **Πλένω τα δόντια μου**, I wash my teeth. The brush is **η βούρτσα** [?].

**Mike:** My brain keeps going to something else. So, με…

**Teacher:** It's the same as before, but now it's not παππούς: it's feminine. **Με τη βούρτσα του** [?]. And the teeth are plural: **τα δόντια του**.

**Mike:** But where does **πλένει** go? That's the part I was focused on. I was trying to shoehorn it in at the end.

**Teacher:** Check the notes. I wrote the whole sentence in the materials.

**Mike:** Ah, it goes first. That changes a lot, and it feels so much simpler.

**Teacher:** Perfect. We'll continue the exercise next time.

**What the lesson produced, in correct Greek:**

- ο ανιψιός · η ανιψιά — nephew · niece
- Λέμε τα νέα της οικογένειάς μας. — We share our family news.
- εκτός γάμου — outside marriage
- Η Γιάννα κάνει γιόγκα. — Yianna does yoga. (a name takes the article)
- κάθε φορά · κάθε μέρα — every time · every day
- δύο φορές τον μήνα — twice a month
- Ο Νάθαν παίζει βιντεοπαιχνίδια με τον παππού του. — Nathan plays video games with his grandpa. (με takes the Target form)
- Πλένει τα δόντια του. — He brushes his teeth. (literally "washes")
