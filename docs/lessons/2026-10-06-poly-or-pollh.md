# Lesson, 6 Oct 2026: πολύ or πολλή

Cleaned from the video-call captions, which had mangled most of the Greek, so the Greek is rewritten in Greek script. **[?]** marks Greek reconstructed from context, and **[…]** marks a stretch that could not be recovered. Filler and the chat before and after the lesson are cut.

**3:08 · Too big**

**Mike:** It's very big. How would I say "too big"?

**Teacher:** We have **παραπολύ** [?]: "too much". Παραπολύ μεγάλο.

**6:20 · This weekend**

**Mike:** How would I say "this weekend"? I feel I can't just say Σαββατοκύριακο.

**Teacher:** You have to use the article too, because it's a specific one: **αυτό το Σαββατοκύριακο**.

**Mike:** Is it the Target form, with "weekend" as well?

**Teacher:** Yes.

**7:16 · Homework: describing a picture**

**Mike:** I think I did the right thing this time. There were four questions. I answered them, then wrote some other sentences.

**Teacher:** Whose parents are they? You describe the picture.

**Mike:** They're my parents: **είναι οι γονείς μου**.

**Teacher:** Good. Μπράβο. Perfect: everything is in the plural. That was on purpose.

**Mike:** It took me about ten minutes, so I felt I needed to do more. The hardest thing was deciding what to say. It was nice that it made me go and check my notes.

**11:00 · πολύ or πολλή**

**Mike:** I've struggled with πολύ. There's πολύ and there are others, and I don't remember which is which.

**Teacher:** We have this one, **πολύ**, and this one, **πολλή**. What's the difference? They sound the same: only in the writing do you see it. I'll give you a sentence and you tell me which one. How would you say "I have a lot of work"?

**Mike:** Work is **δουλειά**. Is it feminine or neuter… singular or plural?

**Teacher:** Feminine, singular.

**Mike:** So **έχω πολλή δουλειά**.

**Teacher:** Mhm. This one we translate as "much" or "many", and this one as "a lot". Now: "I have a lot of things to do."

**Mike:** **Πράγματα**… **Έχω πολλά πράγματα να κάνω.** Why isn't that the future tense?

**Teacher:** It's like English: "I have many things to do", or "I'll do many things", **θα κάνω πολλά πράγματα**. Both are fine.

**19:16 · Find the mistake**

**Teacher:** Yesterday I did an evaluation interview, testing someone's level of Greek. Here's one of her sentences: is it right or wrong? "Έμαθα πολλά πράγματα από τους συμμαθητές μου" [?], I learned many things from my classmates.

**Mike:** **Πολλά** is right, because it's many things, neuter plural.

**Teacher:** Bravo. And the rest?

**Mike:** I'm not seeing anything wrong with it.

**Teacher:** It's not neuter. **Από** is a preposition, and after a preposition we use the Target form. Συμμαθητές is masculine plural, so masculine plural Target form: **από τους συμμαθητές μου**.

**Mike:** That was the very first thing I looked for: a missing article that should be in the Target form.

**22:36 · πολύ, the word that never changes**

**Teacher:** Remember: the second πολύ we call an adverb. It goes with verbs, with adjectives and with other adverbs, and it doesn't change. How would you say "a lot of people"?

**Mike:** Κόσμος…

**Teacher:** **Πολύς κόσμος.** And "a lot of water"?

**Mike:** **Πολύ νερό.** So it stays singular, and it matches the gender.

**Teacher:** If it's masculine or neuter, we write it with υ. If it's feminine, the feminine ending, ή. But if you write πολύ with a feminine, both are accepted, because the language is changing.

**Mike:** So it's technically incorrect, but it's how people write.

**24:36 · Very**

**Teacher:** How would you say "very beautiful"? A very beautiful dog, or child, whatever you want. Keep it simple: no article.

**Mike:** Σκύλος… **πολύ όμορφος σκύλος**.

**Teacher:** And "very well"?

**Mike:** **Πολύ καλά.**

**27:12 · Homework**

**Teacher:** I'll send you something, but do your own research about this too. Make me sentences with it.

**What the lesson produced, in correct Greek:**

- παραπολύ μεγάλο — too big
- αυτό το Σαββατοκύριακο — this weekend
- Είναι οι γονείς μου. — They're my parents.
- Έχω πολλή δουλειά. — I have a lot of work. (δουλειά is feminine)
- Έχω πολλά πράγματα να κάνω. — I have a lot of things to do.
- Έμαθα πολλά πράγματα από τους συμμαθητές μου. — I learned many things from my classmates. (από takes the Target form)
- πολύς κόσμος · πολύ νερό — a lot of people · a lot of water
- πολύ όμορφος σκύλος · πολύ καλά — a very beautiful dog · very well (πολύ before an adjective or adverb never changes)
